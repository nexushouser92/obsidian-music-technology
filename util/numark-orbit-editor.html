Orbit Editor (Unofficial)

A Windows editor for the MIDI mapping of the Numark Orbit controller, written from scratch. It changes the channel, note or CC, mode and color of every pad, plus the knobs and accelerometer of all 4 banks, and applies each change to the Orbit right away. It also turns the Orbit into a 4-track step sequencer.

[!NOTE] This is an unofficial project, not affiliated with or endorsed by Numark or inMusic. It was built for interoperability, without copying any code or assets from the original editor.

Features

  • Automatic linking: finds the Orbit as soon as it is plugged in, by cable or through its wireless receiver. No port to choose.
  • Live preview: every change is stored in the Orbit half a second after your last edit, and the app reads it back to check that it was stored correctly.
  • Follows the Orbit: press a pad on the Orbit and it lights up on screen, selected for editing. The app also follows the bank, the K buttons and the accelerometer.
  • Pads: channel, note, momentary/toggle mode and color, with a color wheel, 3 brightness levels, a 0–3 red·green·blue mix (64 colors) and 12 quick colors.
  • Knobs K1–K4: channel, CC and absolute/relative mode.
  • Accelerometer: channel and CC of each axis (left-right and up-down).
  • Scale mode: 14 scales (major, modes, minors, blues, pentatonics, triads) with root and octave. The 16 pads can be chromatic (scale notes lit) or scale notes only. Each bank has its own scale and lights up in its own colors.
  • Helpers: consecutive notes, same channel, color or mode on the whole bank, and bank copy.
  • Presets: save and open .json (scales included) or .syx (the Orbit's own SysEx dump).
  • English and Spanish, following your Windows language.

Sequencer

Inspired by the sequencer of the nanoKONTROL 2 editor: 4 tracks × 16 steps, where each bank of the Orbit is a track and each pad a step. The pads light up live with the pattern: each track in its color, brighter for louder steps, and the playhead moving across them.

On the Orbit What it does
Pad (press and release) Turns that step on or off
Bank buttons 1–4 Choose the track
Hold a pad + turn the wheel Velocity of that step
Wheel Tempo with the internal clock; when a DAW sends the clock (and so sets the tempo), the velocity of the track
K1–K4 Solo of track 1–4 on or off; both the solos and the K LEDs start off (LED lit = solo)
Right shoulder + tilt forward/back Beat repeat: repeats the current step, faster the more you tilt (1/16, 1/32, 1/48)
Left shoulder + tilt left/right Filter: sends a CC (74, cutoff, by default; set it in the window) on the selected track's channel, to map in a DAW or synth

[!NOTE] The Orbit only reports a K button when you switch to a different K: pressing the same K twice toggles its LED but sends nothing the second time. To take a solo off, go through another K, or use the S buttons in the window (they also bring the LEDs and the solos back in step).

In the window: the note and MIDI channel of each track (drums on channel 10 by default), solo, clear, swing (50–75 %, on 16ths or 8ths), tempo, and Play / Stop. Click a step to turn it on or off, and use the mouse wheel over it to change its velocity.

  • Internal clock: plays on its own through any MIDI output, e.g. a USB synth or the Microsoft GS Wavetable Synth built into Windows.
  • DAW clock: follows your DAW's MIDI clock, Play, Stop and song position through a loopMIDI port (free; the app links to it).

While the sequencer runs, the Orbit holds a sequencer mapping; your own mapping comes back as soon as you stop it or close the app (and on the next start, if the app was closed unexpectedly).

Requirements

  • Windows 10 / 11
  • A Numark Orbit connected by mini-USB cable or through its USB wireless receiver.
  • For the sequencer with a DAW only: loopMIDI.

How to use

  1. Download Orbit.Editor.exe from the Releases page.
  2. Close your DJ software: while the editor is open, the Orbit stays linked to it, and Windows does not let another program use it at the same time.
  3. Run the .exe. The status button turns to Orbit linked and the Orbit's current mapping is loaded.
  4. Click a pad (or press it on the Orbit) and change anything. The Orbit updates by itself.
  5. Use Factory to go back to the factory mapping, and Save… to keep your mapping in a file.

Windows or the antivirus says the app is dangerous

The .exe is not digitally signed, so Windows SmartScreen shows "Unknown publisher". Click More info → Run anyway.

A few antivirus engines flag apps made with PyInstaller as false positives. Every release .exe is built by GitHub Actions from the code in this repository, and each release includes a SHA256.txt so you can check your download:

Get-FileHash "Orbit.Editor.exe" -Algorithm SHA256

Orbit limitations

  • The accelerometer, wheel and K button LEDs have fixed colors in the firmware. Only pads can be colored.
  • The active bank and K can only be changed on the Orbit itself; the firmware ignores such requests from the PC. The app follows the bank and K you choose on the Orbit.
  • The Orbit has no Bluetooth: its wireless link is a 2.4 GHz radio that only works with its USB receiver. See research/WIRELESS.md.
